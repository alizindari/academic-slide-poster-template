Optimization setup

We minimize a differentiable objective, often written as an empirical average:

\[\min_{\vect x\in\mathbb R^d} f(\vect x), \qquad f(\vect x)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n f_i(\vect x), \qquad \vect x^\star\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\vect x}f(\vect x).\]

Gradient descent uses the full gradient; stochastic gradient descent uses an estimate:

\[\text{GD:}\quad \vect x_{t+1}=\vect x_t-\eta_t\nabla f(\vect x_t),\] \[\text{SGD:}\quad \vect x_{t+1}=\vect x_t-\eta_t\vect g_t, \qquad \mathbb E[\vect g_t\mid\vect x_t]=\nabla f(\vect x_t).\]
Goal

Choose the step sizes \(\eta_t\) and iteration budget \(T\) so that optimization error is provably small.

2/15

Geometry of the objective

\(L\)-smoothness controls how quickly the gradient can change:

\[\|\nabla f(\vect x)-\nabla f(\vect y)\|_2 \le L\|\vect x-\vect y\|_2.\]

\(\mu\)-strong convexity gives the objective uniform curvature:

\[f(\vect y)\ge f(\vect x) +\langle\nabla f(\vect x),\vect y-\vect x\rangle +\frac\mu2\|\vect y-\vect x\|_2^2.\]

The ratio \(\kappa=L/\mu\ge1\) is the condition number. Large \(\kappa\) means that different directions have very different curvature.

Hierarchy Strong convexity gives geometric convergence; convexity alone generally gives a sublinear rate.

3/15

One gradient step decreases the objective

Let \(\vect x^+=\vect x-\eta\nabla f(\vect x)\) be one gradient-descent step.

Lemma 1

If \(f\) is \(L\)-smooth, then

\[f(\vect x^+) \le f(\vect x) -\eta\!\left(1-\frac{L\eta}{2}\right) \|\nabla f(\vect x)\|_2^2.\]

In particular, every step with \(0<\eta\le1/L\) satisfies

\[f(\vect x^+)\le f(\vect x) -\frac\eta2\|\nabla f(\vect x)\|_2^2.\]

Meaning Smoothness turns the gradient norm into a certified amount of progress.

4/15

Gradient descent for convex objectives

Assume that \(f\) is convex and \(L\)-smooth, and use a constant step size \(0<\eta\le1/L\).

Theorem 1

For every \(T\ge1\), gradient descent satisfies

\[f(\vect x_T)-f(\vect x^\star) \le\frac{\|\vect x_0-\vect x^\star\|_2^2}{2\eta T}.\]

With \(\eta=1/L\) and \(R=\|\vect x_0-\vect x^\star\|_2\), it is enough to take

\[T\ge\frac{LR^2}{2\varepsilon} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad f(\vect x_T)-f(\vect x^\star)\le\varepsilon.\]

Rate Smooth convex optimization gives \(f(\vect x_T)-f^\star=\mathcal O(1/T)\).

5/15

Strong convexity gives a linear rate

Now suppose that \(f\) is also \(\mu\)-strongly convex and set \(\eta=1/L\).

Theorem 2

Gradient descent contracts the objective gap geometrically:

\[f(\vect x_T)-f(\vect x^\star) \le\left(1-\frac\mu L\right)^T \bigl(f(\vect x_0)-f(\vect x^\star)\bigr) \le e^{-T/\kappa}\bigl(f(\vect x_0)-f^\star\bigr).\]

Well conditioned.
When \(\kappa\approx1\), a small number of full-gradient steps is enough.

Ill conditioned.
When \(\kappa\gg1\), progress is slow along low-curvature directions.

Complexity Reaching error \(\varepsilon\) takes \(\mathcal O\!\left(\kappa\log(1/\varepsilon)\right)\) iterations.

6/15

Stochastic gradients

SGD replaces the full gradient by a conditionally unbiased estimator:

\[\mathbb E[\vect g_t\mid\vect x_t]=\nabla f(\vect x_t), \qquad \mathbb E\!\left[\|\vect g_t-\nabla f(\vect x_t)\|_2^2 \mid\vect x_t\right]\le\sigma^2.\]

The update has a deterministic component and a zero-mean noise component:

\[\vect x_{t+1}=\vect x_t-\eta_t\nabla f(\vect x_t) -\eta_t\underbrace{\bigl(\vect g_t-\nabla f(\vect x_t)\bigr)}_{\text{gradient noise}}.\]

A mini-batch of size \(b\) reduces the variance of an average of independent gradients:

\[\vect g_t=\frac1b\sum_{j=1}^b\nabla f_{i_{t,j}}(\vect x_t), \qquad \mathbb E\|\vect g_t-\nabla f(\vect x_t)\|_2^2\le\frac{\sigma^2}{b}.\]

Trade-off Small batches make cheap progress; large batches produce a more accurate direction.

7/15

The fundamental SGD recursion

Assume that \(f\) is \(L\)-smooth and \(\mu\)-strongly convex. Define

\[a_t=\mathbb E\|\vect x_t-\vect x^\star\|_2^2, \qquad c=\frac{2\mu L}{\mu+L}.\]
Proposition 1

For an unbiased stochastic gradient with variance at most \(\sigma^2\), every step size \(0<\eta_t\le2/(\mu+L)\) satisfies

\[a_{t+1}\le(1-c\eta_t)a_t+\eta_t^2\sigma^2.\]

The two terms have different roles:

\[\underbrace{(1-c\eta_t)a_t}_{\text{contraction toward }\vect x^\star} \quad+\quad \underbrace{\eta_t^2\sigma^2}_{\text{error injected by noise}}.\]

Consequence A useful schedule must preserve contraction while eventually suppressing the noise.

8/15

Diminishing steps converge to the optimum

Use the recursion from Proposition 1 and choose

\[\eta_t=\frac{2}{c(t+t_0)}, \qquad t_0\ge\frac{\mu+L}{c}, \qquad c=\frac{2\mu L}{\mu+L}.\]
Theorem 3

Let

\[C=\max\!\left\{t_0a_0,\frac{4\sigma^2}{c^2}\right\}.\]

Then the expected squared distance obeys

\[\mathbb E\|\vect x_T-\vect x^\star\|_2^2 =a_T\le\frac{C}{T+t_0}.\]

The offset \(t_0\) keeps every early step inside the stable range. The \(1/t\) decay balances contraction against stochastic noise.

Rate Diminishing-step SGD reaches the exact optimum at the sublinear rate \(\mathcal O(1/T)\) in squared distance.

9/15

Constant steps reach a noise floor

Fix \(0<\eta\le2/(\mu+L)\). Iterating the fundamental recursion gives

\[a_T\le(1-c\eta)^T a_0 +\eta^2\sigma^2\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}(1-c\eta)^k.\]
Proposition 2

The geometric sum yields the explicit bound

\[a_T\le(1-c\eta)^T a_0+\frac{\eta\sigma^2}{c}.\]

Consequently,

\[\limsup_{T\to\infty} \mathbb E\|\vect x_T-\vect x^\star\|_2^2 \le\frac{\eta\sigma^2}{c}.\]

Consequence A larger constant step converges faster at first, but settles in a wider neighborhood of the optimum.

Assumptions at a glance

The deterministic and stochastic analyses use the same objective geometry:

\[\mu\mat I\preceq\nabla^2 f(\vect x)\preceq L\mat I \qquad\text{when the Hessian exists}.\]
Equivalent tools
\[\|\nabla f(\vect x)-\nabla f(\vect y)\|_2\le L\|\vect x-\vect y\|_2,\] \[\langle\nabla f(\vect x)-\nabla f(\vect y),\vect x-\vect y\rangle \ge\frac{\mu L}{\mu+L}\|\vect x-\vect y\|_2^2 +\frac{1}{\mu+L}\|\nabla f(\vect x)-\nabla f(\vect y)\|_2^2.\]
  • \(L\) controls the largest stable step; \(\mu\) controls contraction.
  • \(\sigma^2\) measures stochastic-gradient variance; \(b\) is the mini-batch size.
  • \(\kappa=L/\mu\) summarizes conditioning.
12/15

Proof of the descent lemma

Smoothness gives a quadratic upper model around \(\vect x\):

\[f(\vect y)\le f(\vect x) +\langle\nabla f(\vect x),\vect y-\vect x\rangle +\frac L2\|\vect y-\vect x\|_2^2.\]
Substitute the update

Set \(\vect y=\vect x^+=\vect x-\eta\nabla f(\vect x)\). Then

\[\langle\nabla f(\vect x),\vect x^+-\vect x\rangle =-\eta\|\nabla f(\vect x)\|_2^2,\] \[\|\vect x^+-\vect x\|_2^2 =\eta^2\|\nabla f(\vect x)\|_2^2.\]

Collecting both terms produces the exact one-step decrease.

Result
\[f(\vect x^+)\le f(\vect x) -\eta\left(1-\frac{L\eta}{2}\right) \|\nabla f(\vect x)\|_2^2.\]
13/15

Proof of linear convergence

Strong convexity implies the Polyak–Łojasiewicz inequality

\[\|\nabla f(\vect x)\|_2^2 \ge2\mu\bigl(f(\vect x)-f(\vect x^\star)\bigr).\]

Combine With \(\eta=1/L\), the descent lemma gives \(\displaystyle f(\vect x_{t+1})-f^\star \le(1-\mu/L)\bigl(f(\vect x_t)-f^\star\bigr)\).

Iterate

Applying the contraction \(T\) times yields \(\displaystyle f(\vect x_T)-f^\star \le(1-1/\kappa)^T\bigl(f(\vect x_0)-f^\star\bigr)\).

14/15

What mini-batching changes

Average \(b\) independent stochastic gradients at the same iterate:

\[\vect g_t^{(b)}=\frac1b\sum_{j=1}^b\vect g_{t,j}, \qquad \mathbb E\|\vect g_t^{(b)}-\nabla f(\vect x_t)\|_2^2 \le\frac{\sigma^2}{b}.\]

The mean update direction is unchanged:

\[\mathbb E[\vect g_t^{(b)}\mid\vect x_t]=\nabla f(\vect x_t).\]

The fundamental recursion becomes

\[a_{t+1}\le(1-c\eta_t)a_t+\frac{\eta_t^2\sigma^2}{b}.\]

For a constant step size, the limiting term is

\[\limsup_{T\to\infty}a_T\le\frac{\eta\sigma^2}{bc}.\]
Practical message

Mini-batching lowers the noise floor by \(1/b\), but each iteration uses \(b\) gradient evaluations. It is most valuable when those evaluations run efficiently in parallel.
